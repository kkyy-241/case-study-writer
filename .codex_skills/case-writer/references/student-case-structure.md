# Student-Facing Case Structure

## Required Sections

Use section titles that fit the case. The following sequence works well for finance-focused entrepreneurship cases:

For this repository, use `references/format-templates/安德科铭正文案例.pdf` as the default student-case format template. Match its document style and organization unless the user requests a different template.

The student-facing case body should be 8,000-10,000 Chinese characters unless the user explicitly changes the length target.

1. Title
2. Opening decision scene
3. Entrepreneur and company background
4. Industry and market environment
5. Product/service and business model
6. Entrepreneurial development timeline
7. Financing need and causes
8. Financing alternatives, valuation dispute, control conflict, or investor bargaining
9. Equity structure and financing instruments
10. Important facts, data, and exhibits for class analysis
11. Open decision ending
12. Five discussion questions
13. Footer-style endnotes for sources from the `background` folder

## Opening Decision Scene

The first page should quickly answer:

- Who is deciding?
- Where and when is the decision being made?
- What financing or control decision is on the table?
- Why is the decision urgent?
- What options seem plausible?

Examples of strong decision scenes:

- Founder staring at three term sheets with different valuations and control terms.
- CFO reviewing a cash runway projection before choosing debt, VC, bridge financing, or strategic investor.
- Founder team debating whether to accept a high valuation with strict redemption/ratchet terms.
- Entrepreneur deciding whether to build a holding platform, employee option pool, or founder-control arrangement before financing.

## Body Requirements

For each major fact, explain why it matters financially:

- Technology or product complexity -> information asymmetry, diligence difficulty, valuation uncertainty.
- Capital-intensive production -> external financing need, staged investment, debt unsuitability.
- Fast growth -> working capital, runway, dilution, timing of rounds.
- Strategic investor -> resources and market access, but possible control or exclusivity concerns.
- VC term sheet -> risk allocation, governance, downside protection, incentives.
- Founder equity structure -> control rights, future financing capacity, team incentives.
- Latest important news -> changed financing urgency, valuation, investor bargaining power, regulatory risk, market adoption, reputation, or exit options.

## Finance Content To Include When Available

- Financing round table: date, investor, amount, valuation, instrument, share percentage, key terms, use of proceeds.
- Cap table before and after financing.
- Cash runway: cash, monthly burn, planned spending, revenue/cash inflow, funding gap.
- Valuation support: comparable firms, revenue/profit multiples, DCF assumptions, scenario analysis, option value, strategic value.
- Contract terms: preferred shares, convertible notes, SAFE-like instruments, warrants, redemption, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, ratchets, earn-outs, veto rights, board seats.
- Control arrangements: founder holding platform, limited partnership GP/LP roles, voting agreement, option pool, reserved matters.

## Discussion Questions

Write exactly five unless the user requests otherwise. Questions must:

- Follow a progression from facts to theory to decision.
- Focus on the core financing contradiction.
- Require finance tools, not generic management opinions.
- Be answerable from the case plus reasonable assumptions.
- Invite debate where alternatives have tradeoffs.

Recommended progression:

1. Diagnose the financing need and stage: What created the funding gap, and what risks does the company face?
2. Analyze financing choice: Which source/instrument best fits the company's stage, risk, assets, and information environment?
3. Analyze valuation and terms: How should the company evaluate valuation, dilution, downside protections, and investor value-add?
4. Analyze control and governance: How should founders design equity structure, voting/control rights, board rights, and incentives?
5. Make a decision: Which option should the protagonist choose, under what assumptions, and what safeguards should be negotiated?

## Ending

End at the decision moment. Do not summarize the correct answer. Close with the protagonist weighing competing options and needing to decide.

Before writing the ending, check that the case still leaves a meaningful unresolved choice for students. If the latest news has already resolved the historical decision, frame the student case at an earlier decision date or explicitly ask the user whether a retrospective case is desired.

## Source Notes

Write the student case in Chinese unless the user explicitly requests another language. Mark facts drawn from `background` with numbered note markers. Place concise source notes as page-footer notes when the output format supports them; otherwise place an endnote block labeled `资料来源尾注` at the end of the PDF draft. Include the source file/title, publisher or website when available, and date or access date when available.
